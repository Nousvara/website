Unlearn First — how we think

Every tool here comes out of the same way of working. It has a name — Unlearn First — and one line holds the whole of it: first look, then think, then do, and check whether it actually worked. Below is the shape of that thinking. It's the reason the tools are focused, private, and honest instead of whatever was easiest to ship.

The shape

Understand it, solve it, grow from it

Eight steps in three moves. Three steps to understand a problem, three to solve it, two to grow from what happened — then it repeats. We remember it as 3-3-2.

Understand it

1 · Observe  →  2 · Question  →  3 · Think

Notice what's really there before reacting. Ask the sharp question, not the comfortable one. Then weigh the options and decide a direction — analysis and choice, not just analysis.

Solve it

4 · Plan  →  5 · Act  →  6 · Validate

Prepare the approach, build the smallest honest version, then check it against reality — not against how clever it felt to make. Imperfect action beats perfect planning that never ships.

Grow from it

7 · Reflect  →  8 · Adapt

Look back at what worked and what didn't, then improve — and carry the thinking to the next problem. The first version is never the final one, and the cycle starts again.

The one rule that outranks the rest: keep moving. The common trap is getting stuck understanding a problem and never acting on it. A full pass through all eight steps — even a rough one — beats a perfect first step that never reaches "act."

In full

The eight steps

Each step is one honest question you answer before moving on. The depth scales with the problem — a quick task gets a mental note, a big decision gets real thought.

01

Observe

What do I actually see — facts, not assumptions? And am I calm enough to think clearly right now?

02

Question

Is this the real problem or a symptom? If the question feels safe and obvious, I haven't gone deep enough.

03

Think

What are the options, and which fits? Has someone solved this already? Can the people affected actually adopt it?

04

Plan

What exactly will I do first, what does success look like, and what's my backup if it goes wrong?

05

Act

Am I actually doing it, or still planning? Is the output clear enough that someone else could use it?

06

Validate

Does it really work, tested against reality? If AI helped, did I verify the facts independently?

07

Reflect

What worked, what didn't, what would I do differently? And is this still worth continuing at all?

08

Adapt

How is the next version better, and where else does this same thinking apply? Then repeat.

Throughout

Three principles, active at every step

The eight steps are what we do. These three are how we do them — they run underneath the whole cycle.

Unlearn first

At every step, ask "what am I assuming that might be wrong?" — including our own comfortable defaults. It's the habit the whole method is named for.

Use AI wisely

AI helps at every step, but you direct it and you verify it. Think with AI, not let it think for you. It's confident even when it's wrong — the judgment stays human.

Seek human input

Get what AI can't give — empathy, lived context, genuine disagreement, accountability. The sharpest judgment often comes from someone who's actually lived your problem.

One honest note the method insists on: solve a problem, but sit with a feeling. Grief, fear and loss aren't problems to fix — they're feelings to go through. The framework applies softer here: name it honestly, take one small step, and talk to a real person.
